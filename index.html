Important: your targets should progress

The original session list repeats some pace and watt ranges. Treat those as starting targets, not fixed numbers for the whole season. Every 4–6 weeks, review your training data with your coach and update the zones if your fitness has genuinely improved.

Example: if your run threshold improves from 4:25/km to 4:18/km, your threshold-based interval targets should be recalculated from 4:18/km. If your FTP rises from 243 W to 255 W, your bike power zones and interval targets should be recalculated from 255 W. The plan should follow your tested fitness, not make you chase a predicted number.

Daily nutrition and fuelling guide

Your food intake should change with your training load. Each session in the week-by-week plan has its own carbohydrate, protein, calorie-safety and fuelling guide underneath it. These are practical starting guides, not strict limits or a diet. Eat enough to support training, recovery and normal growth.

About calories: I haven't put a fixed calorie target on each day or invented daily calorie numbers. At 16, your energy needs include growth and development as well as exercise, and a formula cannot reliably account for both. Don't set a calorie ceiling or try to make yourself fit a number. Ask a sports dietitian experienced with adolescent endurance athletes to estimate your needs with your parent/guardian and coach.

Fat and overall energy

Include sources of fat every day, such as olive oil, avocado, nuts, seeds, eggs and oily fish. Don't deliberately reduce fat to make room for a macro target. Your total energy intake should be enough to maintain health, growth and training adaptation; a qualified sports dietitian can help personalise this.

Fuelling during sessions

  • For shorter, easy sessions, water and normal meals may be enough.
  • For sessions lasting around 60–90 minutes or longer, especially hard sessions, practise taking carbohydrate during exercise. A common starting point is 30–60 g per hour, adjusted with your coach and gut tolerance.
  • For long sessions, plan fluids and regular carbohydrate rather than waiting until you feel exhausted.
  • After training, have a meal or snack containing both carbohydrate and protein, especially if another session is coming up.

How to use the plan safely

  • Keep Tuesday technique swim and Friday speed-run group as planned, but follow your coach's adjustments.
  • Don't force the peak volumes or try to increase swim, bike, and run load all at once.
  • Easy days must stay easy. If fatigue, pain, illness, poor sleep, or performance decline builds up, reduce training and speak to your coach and parent/guardian.
  • At 16, consistent recovery, enough food, hydration, and sleep are part of the training plan. A qualified youth endurance coach should review the overall load.
  • Never change a target just to hit a number. Progress is shown by repeatable performance, good technique, and recovering well.

Hourly fuelling is a practice guide, not a strict target. Start at the lower end, practise in training, and adjust for duration, intensity, weather and stomach comfort. At 16, discuss longer-session fuelling with your parent/guardian and coach or a sports dietitian; do not push above 60 g/hour without individual professional guidance. Check sports products are suitable for your dietary needs.
